Changelog

  • 2026-08-06: Tapping one of the tip bubbles that greets a new player no longer plays a move. Three of those bubbles sit over the board, and although they look like something you can tap away, the tap went straight through to the point underneath — on a computer, where a single click plays a move, that answered the problem there and then, almost always wrongly, before the tip had even been read. Tapping the bubble now only dismisses it. Tapping anywhere else still dismisses it and does whatever you tapped, so nothing else about them changes. This only ever affected players new enough to be shown these bubbles, in their first few problems.
  • 2026-08-06: The screen shown after an answer no longer reports a change that disagrees with the answer. Both numbers it can show — the rank movement, and the change in repeat delay after a review problem — come from estimates the app is still refining, and either could occasionally land on the wrong side of zero: a small rank gain after a wrong answer, or a review pushed further away after failing it. Either now reads as no change rather than contradicting the result you just got, and a rank line is shown after every answer instead of being left out when it happened to be zero. This is only what the screen says: your rank and your review schedule are unchanged, and still exactly what the app worked out.
  • 2026-08-05: The rank scale down the side of the Rank tab's chart was labelled half a rank too low, so the graph read a whole rank stronger than it should — a player at high 3 dan saw their line sitting in a stripe marked 4 dan. The labels were placed for an older way of writing ranks, where a whole rank was centred on its number; the app now treats those numbers as the boundaries between ranks, and the chart had not been moved with it. The gridlines now fall on the boundaries and each rank is named in the middle of its own stripe, so the chart agrees with the rank stated underneath it and with the front page. Only the chart's scale was affected — no rank, and nothing the app did with it, was ever wrong.
  • 2026-08-05: Your very first problem is now chosen by the same rule as every later one, and solving it moves your rank straight away. A brand-new account was handed a problem at exactly the rank it was showing you — the one case where the app aimed at what it was displaying rather than slightly above it — so the first correct answer moved the estimate without ever moving the number on screen, which looked like the answer had been ignored. New accounts now start on something a little harder, and while you have not missed anything yet the app reaches a couple of ranks further still. A first correct answer now moves the shown rank by a full rank. The extra reach ends the moment you miss a problem, so it only ever applies while your record is clean.
  • 2026-08-05: Anyone much stronger than the rank they start at now reaches their real rank in roughly half as many problems. The app deliberately serves a little harder than the rank it is showing you, so that it keeps learning where you actually are — but how much harder was a fixed five ranks, regardless of how much or how little it knew about you. That reach is now earned: one rank for every problem you clear in a row, reset whenever you miss one. The first problems are gentler than before as a result — a brand-new account is offered something at its own level rather than five ranks above it — while a run of correct answers lets the app climb to meet you much faster. A strong player starting from scratch now reaches a dan rank in around twenty-five problems rather than forty. Your rank itself, and the deliberately cautious way it is shown while still uncertain, are unchanged.
  • 2026-08-05: Added a methodology page — the full account of how a problem's difficulty is measured, how those difficulties become the rank you are shown, and how the weakness dimensions are found and used. It is a technical read rather than a summary, written for anyone who wants the reasoning behind the numbers. It is the project's own working document, published straight from the source with each release, so it cannot drift out of date with the app. English only; linked from the About page.
  • 2026-08-04: The bubble that greets a new player on their very first problem now describes how moves are actually played on a touchscreen: tap the point, then tap it again to confirm. On a phone or tablet the first tap only places an outlined stone — a second tap on the same point plays it, and a tap elsewhere moves it — but the bubble described only the first tap, so a first-time player could be left tapping once and wondering why nothing happened. On a computer, where a single click plays the move, the wording is unchanged.
  • 2026-08-04: The rank on the front page is worked out a new way, aimed at the complaint that it used to jump around and read too high in your first sessions. It now starts from a measured picture of how strong onlymove's players actually are and updates that with your answers, instead of starting from wherever your own estimate happened to have drifted to — which is what let an early run of right or wrong answers carry itself forward. While it is still unsure of you it deliberately shows the cautious end of its estimate rather than the middle, so the number climbs towards your real rank as evidence comes in instead of overshooting and having to walk back down; once it is confident, the caution disappears and the shown rank settles on the estimate itself. Problem selection follows the same number: problems are chosen a little harder than what is shown, so the app keeps learning about you, but never more than five ranks above it — you will not be handed dan problems while it is calling you a beginner. The Rank tab's front-page-rank line now traces exactly what you were shown, on OnlyMove and on every server option; the steadier skill line beside it is unchanged and still shows the underlying estimate.
  • 2026-08-02: Just under 3% of the collection has been withdrawn — 4,708 problems whose answer did not survive being played out. Every problem is checked at creation by playing both the winning reply and the best losing reply through to the end of the game, but the move-by-move evaluations stored in the problem file come from separate, shallower readings, and a sweep of the whole collection found places where the two disagree: the winning line finishing in a loss, or the refutation of a wrong move finishing in a win. Those problems are gone, and any attempts you had on them have gone with them, so your totals and history will show slightly fewer solves than before. The disagreement was far more common on 13×13 than on 9×9, since those lines run longer and turn on ko much more often — exactly where a quick reading and a deep one part company. A further 348 problems kept their place but had their main line completed: it stopped short of the finish, usually one pass before the game was actually over, so stepping through the answer ran out of moves early. Difficulties and the Weaknesses tab's skill dimensions were refitted on what remains, so your rank may shift slightly as that feeds through.
  • 2026-07-28: Every problem's difficulty has been refitted against the latest solving record, and the Weaknesses tab's skill dimensions were relearned from it. A new dimension separates killing — positions whose answer is the move that takes an opponent group off the board — from ko threats, positions that turn on finding a threat elsewhere. Two dimensions that shipped previously, attack/defence and reading/shape, no longer matched how players differ once the difficulties were refreshed and have been retired; as always they are kept on file and return by themselves if they reappear in future data. Separately, the app's internal rank scale was rebuilt onto a single consistent footing — every rank you see reads exactly as before, but the app rebuilds its locally stored data and re-learns your personal review and weakness models the first time you open it after this update. Your rank may shift slightly as the refreshed difficulties feed through.
  • 2026-07-28: Once you get a review right, the next sight of that problem now comes about twice as far apart. Checked against the solving record, reviews were coming back too soon after a first success — they were being cleared around 80% of the time and above, where the schedule aims for nearer 70%, meaning they arrived before you had really begun to forget. The delay after a missed problem was already right and is unchanged. The cause was that the app treated "you just missed this" and "you just got this right" as two steps of one ladder rather than the different situations they are: getting a missed problem right again restores more than an ordinary success's worth of memory, and that is now accounted for.
  • 2026-07-27: Reviews of failed problems now come round somewhat more often. The pace is still set by how much you play and how quickly you personally forget, but it is now matched to a lighter day than your recent average rather than to the average itself — so a spell of playing less than usual no longer leaves due reviews piling up, and at your normal pace you get through them with room to spare.
  • 2026-07-27: Every problem's difficulty has been refitted against the latest solving record, and the Weaknesses tab gained a new skill dimension separating attack from defence — one side covers positions whose answer is to press an advantage, the other positions whose answer is to save what you have. It takes the place of a dimension that no longer matched how players actually differ; the old one is kept on file and returns by itself if it reappears in future data. Your rank may shift slightly as the refreshed difficulties feed through, and the app re-derives your stored history on first load.
  • 2026-07-27: The Community tab's rank distribution is now built from each player's actual rank, the same estimate the front page shows them and the same one the leaderboard ranks them by. It used to be inferred from the difficulty of the problems people had been served that week, which is only approximately their level: the app deliberately serves slightly above your rank while it is still unsure of it, so newer players read too strong, and the difficulty was rounded to the band of a whole problem set, which reads strong players too weak. The chart also now counts the players who carry a rank — the same 30-problem threshold the leaderboard uses — rather than anyone who answered a handful of problems in the week, so it no longer mixes in players whose rank isn't yet established. The country list on the same tab now shows every country instead of the top fifteen and an "Other" row.
  • 2026-07-26: The Weaknesses tab now lists fewer, more trustworthy issues. Each candidate issue used to be judged on its own against a fixed confidence bar, so a borderline one was listed whenever it cleared that bar — and since every skill dimension is checked for every player, and the app gains dimensions as the problem collection grows, the list would slowly fill with entries that were right only about four times in five. The listed issues now share one error budget: they are added strongest-first for as long as the combined chance that any of them is spurious stays small. A single strong finding is listed exactly as before; a long run of marginal ones is now cut short.
  • 2026-07-26: Problem selection and rank convergence now account for how guessable each problem is. A problem you can stumble into by luck tells the app less about your strength than a sharp one you clear just as often, and that was previously ignored in two places: guessable problems were slightly over-weighted when choosing your next problem, and the app's threshold for "your rank is now pinned down" assumed a precision no amount of solving could actually reach. The second one had a visible effect — problems kept being served a fraction of a rank above your level indefinitely, instead of settling onto your own rank, and the switch to weakness-targeted problems came later than intended. Both now use each problem's full response curve.
  • 2026-07-23: Moves can now be played from a physical keyboard, as an accessibility feature for anyone who finds precise tapping or mouse work difficult: type a move's board coordinates — e.g. C3 — anywhere in the app and press Enter to play it (columns skip I, rows count from the bottom; works while solving and while reviewing). To make coordinates readable, tiny coordinate labels now line the very edge of the board, small enough to stay clear of stones on the first line. Nothing changes visually for anyone else — the entry field itself is invisible.
  • 2026-07-23: Offline play now lasts much longer: when the problems near your rank run out and the next problem set can't be downloaded, the app now serves problems from the sets already stored on your device — further from your usual level — instead of silently showing the same problem again after each answer. A brief notice appears when this fallback starts, and another when normal at-your-level serving resumes; only if nothing stored remains unsolved does the app re-serve your last problem, now with a clear explanation instead of looking like a bug. Answers to such a re-served problem were already excluded from your rank and stats.
  • 2026-07-22: Play Store (Android) app: pressing Notify now brings up Android's own notification permission dialog directly (app version 3). The web app asks the app to request the permission itself instead of relying on Chrome's broken relay, so the manual settings walkthrough from earlier today is only shown on older app versions that can't do this yet — and if Android would silently refuse the dialog (permission previously denied, or notifications turned off in settings), the app opens the right settings screen for you instead.
  • 2026-07-22: The result overlay after an answer now appears immediately. Previously two things could delay it by up to a few seconds: after a review (repeat) problem, the retraining that computes the "repeat delay" line ran before the overlay (it now runs behind it, and the line fills in as soon as it's ready); and after solving a new problem, picking your next challenge — sometimes including a problem-set download — also ran before the overlay (it now happens under it).
  • 2026-07-22: In the Play Store (Android) version, enabling review reminders now also walks you through allowing notifications for the app in Android's settings. Android 13+ blocks an app's notifications until you allow them, and the system dialog that should ask on the Notify press doesn't appear (a known Android/Chrome issue) — so reminders were silently dropped and the app "forgot" the Notify setting within a day. Until that's fixed upstream, the app now shows the steps to allow notifications right after you press Notify; with the toggle on, reminders both arrive and stay enabled.
  • 2026-07-22: New players who miss three of their first ten problems now get a small tip bubble: the correct answer is usually the natural response to the opponent's last move — an encouragement not to over-analyze the early problems.
  • 2026-07-21: The leaderboard got a units selector — view the board in onlymove, EGF, or AGA units. The former Declared column is now Verified: it shows only the official rating of a player's linked EGD or AGA account, refreshed weekly from those databases — self-declared survey ranks are no longer shown on the board. A rating appears only while it is active by the EGF's activity criteria: a tournament within 2 years for dan players, 1 year for single-digit kyu, 6 months for double-digit kyu. Every value reads as the rank with progress toward the next — e.g. a 2389 GoR shows as (39%) 4d, the same style as the front-page rank list — and with EGF or AGA units selected, the onlymove estimate is converted into that federation's own scale for a direct side-by-side comparison; in onlymove units the Verified column stays empty. On phones the page now opens zoomed out to fit the screen width exactly.
  • 2026-07-20: You can now link an American Go Association (AGA) rating to your account, the same way as a European (EGD) rating — through an invite link. The invite tool now has a single search that queries both the EGD and AGA databases at once and lists the results together, each labelled by source; pick a player and their current rank is taken straight from that database (refreshed weekly).   • 2026-07-20: You can now hide your profile from the leaderboard — a control at the bottom of the page withholds your name, country, and declared ranks (your onlymove rank still appears, but anonymously), and can be switched off again at any time. New axes are added automatically as the data grows.
