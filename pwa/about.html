About onlymove.net

This project was created by Henry Hemming and started on July 12th, 2025.

Problem Details

  • The ruleset is Japanese, with a 6.5 point komi.
  • Problems are generated by KataGo during self-play with marginally suboptimal moves.
  • To ensure quality, each problem is checked by the KataGo model kata9x9-b18c384nbt-20231025. The model uses 1,000 playouts to verify that the best move wins, and another 1,000 playouts to verify no other move wins.
  • Two board sizes are available: 9×9 at onlymove.net/9 and 13×13 at onlymove.net/13. The collection contains over 141,000 unique problems (about 122,000 on 9×9 and 20,000 on 13×13). Each problem can be displayed in any of eight orientations.
  • Each problem's difficulty is first predicted from KataGo's internal read of the position (the same representation used for the weakness motifs) together with how well a range of KataGo configurations solve it — including b18c384nbt-humanv0 at various rank settings and kata9x9-b18c384nbt-20231025 at various temperatures. That prediction is then corrected by real players' first-time attempts: a problem people find harder or easier than predicted is nudged accordingly, while problems with few attempts stay close to the prediction. A single model rates both board sizes on one shared scale.
  • A problem's rating is the player strength at which it is solved about 80% of the time, not 50% — so the rating reflects the level needed to clear it reliably rather than to merely have a coin-flip chance.
  • To turn ratings into ranks, players are occasionally asked their rank on the servers they play on. These declarations anchor the rating-to-rank curve to the European Go Database scale and calibrate how each server's ranks line up with ours, so the rank you see corresponds to a real-world rank rather than an abstract number.

How accurate are the ratings?

The rating model is checked a few ways — most stringently by holding 10% of the problems out of training entirely, so the model never sees a single human result on them (figures below with a ± are averaged over three independent training runs on such a hold-out).

  • Rank vs. real-world rank. Comparing each player's model rating against the rank they declared on other servers gives a Spearman rank correlation of about 0.83 (± 0.01), across roughly 200 declarations from about 110 players. In plain terms: order players by our rating and you get very nearly the same order as their actual rank.
  • Reading difficulty from the position. Because the app only ever serves problems matched to your rank (around an 80% clear rate), that narrow band hides how much the model really knows — almost anything predicts well there. Judged across the full range of difficulties instead, from trivial to far too hard, the difficulty it reads from KataGo's view of a position lets it put, on average, a 61% probability on the outcome that actually happens — solve or fail — versus 55% for a baseline that knows only the overall solve rate (roughly an 18% reduction in prediction error). That gap is its real value for placing brand-new problems nobody has ever attempted.
  • Even across the difficulty range. That prediction quality stays roughly constant from the easiest problems to the hardest, rather than being accurate in the middle and off at the extremes.

Ranking System

  • Your rank changes quickly at first, but rank change based on each tsumego result slows with each problem you fail and speeds up when you answer correctly. With a cap at how stable a rank can get, which means there is a limit how fast your rank can decrease but not how fast it can increase.
  • Once your rank stabilizes, it will take roughly 10 wins to go up a rank or 10 losses to go down one.
  • The rank on the front page is your live rank, nudged up and down by that momentum as you solve. The stats screen's Rank tab also plots a separate historical rank curve, computed differently: a maximum-likelihood estimate of your strength over time, fitted across your whole solving history at once rather than carried forward result-by-result. Because it is not path-dependent, it reads your trajectory cleanly in hindsight (with a band showing how precisely each point is known), so the two can differ — most visibly during onboarding, when the live rank is still catching up.
  • Even high rank problems can be guessed correctly, but not frequently enough (4/5) to maintain your rank.
  • To help you learn, problems you fail are shown again later, whenever the system thinks you have 80% chance of remembering the solution.
  • Answering problems that are from links and not part of your own progression does not affect your rank.

Features

  • It can be installed from your browser as a standalone app.
  • Your progress is tied to a personal account code, shown under the Account menu. Enter the same code on another device to keep your solving history and rank in sync everywhere you play.
  • The review mode has numerous positions pre-evaluated by kata9x9-b18c384nbt-20231025 with a minimum of 30 playouts, usually hundreds of playouts.
  • For positions that have not been pre-evaluated, it uses a server to provide kata9x9-b18c384nbt-20231025 with 1 playout to analyze positions.
  • Upon failure, if you played too fast and you get better results when you think for longer, a slow red screen will appear.
  • Upon failure, if you failed a repeat problem, for which you didnt spend much time reviewing, and you get better results when you spend more time reviewing the solution, a slow red screen will appear.
  • Problem selection learns from your attempt history. Every position is mapped to 64 motif axes that summarize KataGo's internal view of the board, and a per-user logistic regression tilts upcoming problems toward motifs that have been tripping you up. When you pick the wrong move, the move you actually played is mapped into the same space, so the system can distinguish "the position is hard to read" from "you over- or under-play this kind of move".
  • Push notifications can be enabled to remind you when failed problems are due for review.
  • A Community tab shows anonymous server-wide statistics over the past week — overall activity, the rank distribution, time usage, and how quickly players return to failed problems — with your own position highlighted.
  • The statistics page tracks factors influencing your problem-solving probability. Ideally, time-related metrics should be close to zero, while a positive trend in the "per problem solved" metric indicates learning. It also shows a per-attempt rating-history chart, a smoothed long-term skill estimate with a 95% confidence band, a session-to-session form variance metric, and a sorted list of motifs you tend to misplay described in plain sentences.
  • You can see your estimated rank on various Go servers — how your strength would read on each server's own scale, adjusted for your typical solving speed — from the Rank tab's server selector or by tapping your front-page rank.
